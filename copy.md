## Strategy

- Mass desire: the feeling on the drive home from SOL (shoulders down, phone off, calm) and the wish to keep it. Underneath that: a self care ritual that is actually kept, and belonging to a place that feels like yours. Secondary desires: value (going more often for less) and sharing it (a friend, a partner).
- Awareness: product aware. Traffic is Meta and Instagram, email to past guests (the brand has 343 Google reviews and tens of thousands of visits behind it), and the SOL site. The reader knows SOL or knows bathhouses; the news is "every week" and the price. The headline therefore names the product and the price (Schwartz, stage 1 to 2 awareness) and selects the reader with the sentence already in their head.
- Sophistication: stage 3 to 4. Brisbane and the Gold Coast have five or more bathhouses selling "soak, steam, plunge" at $40 to $75. The claim alone no longer sells. The copy leads with the mechanism (a standing weekly reservation, on a lake, with no protocols) and qualifies the reader ("for everyone who said I need to do this more often").
- USP: the only bathhouse in South East Queensland floating on a rainforest lake, 50 minutes from both cities, adults only and phone free; and a membership priced so a Saturday costs what a Wednesday costs.
- Mechanism: one session every week, any day, booked in a few taps; no protocols, so the 90 minutes becomes a ritual of your own rather than a circuit to complete; a fixed weekly price that removes the weekend surcharge and the "should I" from every visit.
- Honest admission (Ogilvy): it only pays if you come. The copy says so, and sends the once a year visitor to a casual booking.
- Biggest objections, in order: "Will I actually go every week?", "$59.95 a week adds up", "Is it crowded?", "Is it worth the drive?", "Can I use it on weekends?", "What if I miss a week?", "Can I cancel?", "Change rooms and phones", "I'm pregnant / I have a condition".
- One action: reserve the membership (form). Repeated after the offer, after the value table, after the proof, and at the close.

## Executive summary

**Awareness and sophistication.** The page is written for a product aware reader on a stage 3 to 4 market. Meta, Instagram and past guest email traffic already knows SOL or knows bathhouses, so the headline names the product and the price ("Now it's $59.95 a week") and selects the reader with the sentence already in their head ("You said you'd do this more often"). That is Collier's entering the conversation and Schwartz's identification in one line. South East Queensland has at least five bathhouses selling the same soak, steam, plunge claim at $40 to $75, so the claim alone cannot carry the page; the mechanism does: a standing weekly reservation, on a lake, with no protocols, at a price that makes Saturday cost what Wednesday costs.

**Techniques.** The drive home section is pure Collier: the first paragraph is the feeling the reader already has, in the order they had it. The pain list is Sugarman's slippery slope into the offer. The price card follows Bly's bullet rules (benefit first, feature as support), and the value block follows Schwartz's "compare and prove": the $115 Saturday against $59.95, and 52 visits priced both ways. The honest admission ("It only makes sense if you come") is Ogilvy's weakness first, placed right under the price so the strength that follows is believed. Proof sits at the three decision points: the hero proof bar, the six verbatim Google reviews before the founders' story, and the rating label on the form section. The "who it is not for" list does double duty as compliance (pregnancy, adults only) and as trust.

**Psychology of the key choices.** The headline leads with "you said" rather than "SOL" because the reader's own words are the highest trust source on the page. "$59.95" appears seven times because price is the objection, not the benefit; the page makes it boring by repetition and then beats it with the Saturday comparison. The use case cards ("Monday reset", "Saturday with your sister") are Schwartz's "put claims in action": the reader sees a week, not a feature list. The helicopter and the suites sit after the offer, not before, so they lift the brand without distracting from the one action. "No protocols" is the brand's own phrase and the page's quiet mechanism: a ritual you own is a ritual you keep.

**What to tighten.** Four things are assumptions that must be confirmed before a dollar of traffic: any day validity, the 15 percent treatment discount, the Bring a Friend pass, and the pause and cancel terms (marked ASSUMED throughout). The proof would be stronger with two member quotes (nobody is a member yet; collect them in month one and swap them into slots 1 and 5). The CTA worth testing is "Reserve My Membership" against "Hold My Week", which carries the mechanism in the button. And the hero would convert harder with a short vertical video of the fire pit at dusk behind the headline, if the client has footage; the still is strong but motion sells water.
